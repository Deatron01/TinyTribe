TinyTribe: training the villagers

Every network that was trained and how it did, built from the experiments' own files on 2026-10-12 10:04. 47 experiments.

A villager sees an 11 x 11 patch of the map and a vector about itself and its village, and chooses one of 21 actions each tick. One network, shared by all villagers, was trained in stages: by imitating recorded play, by being corrected on its own play, and by learning from what the game pays for outcomes. Each stage was judged the same way: the network runs whole villages for a year on maps nothing was trained on, and the scripted bots and blind choice play the same maps.

why the first run of learning from outcomes made the network worsenote

The network that could keep a village alive for 17 days came out of 300 updates of PPO lasting 4.6. Its numbers show two mistakes in the method, not in the idea. First: for the opening ten updates only the value estimate was meant to learn, but policy and value shared one network, so the value error ran back through the whole of it. By update 11, before the policy had been trained at all, it already differed from its starting self by 1.4 nats: what imitation had taught was overwritten by a critic that knew nothing yet. Second: the first real policy update then moved the policy by a KL of 0.15 with 47% of its choices outside the clipping range, many times what PPO is meant to allow. The trainer was rewritten: the critic is a network of its own that reads what the policy's LSTM holds but sends nothing back into it; the policy learns at a third of the rate, an update stops once the policy has moved a KL of 0.015, the clip is 0.1 instead of 0.2 and the entropy bonus is a third of what it was. In a trial of the new trainer the policy did not move at all during the critic's warm-up and by a KL of about 0.0005 per update after it.

In the bots' villages that advice is 240 ticks in 3.7 million. Here the loss weighs every action together with its argument, so the pickaxe, the chest, the fire and the tent each count for themselves and not as one craft_build among the cooked fish.

Why. Trained only on villages the bots ran, the network had never seen where its own slips lead. Here it plays, the bot says on every tick what it would have done in that villager's place, and those situations are added to the training data. The bot's advice. It keeps a villager's plan only while the villager does as advised; when the villager does something else the advice starts again from the situation, so it never hangs on a plan the network left and cannot see. Rare actions are weighted up in the loss (power 0.75). Setup.
